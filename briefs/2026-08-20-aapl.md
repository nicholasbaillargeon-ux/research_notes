## Thesis

The market is pricing Apple as a compounding platform business that just proved it can still grow double digits at scale: June-quarter revenue hit $109.4B, up 16% year over year, with iPhone, Mac and Services all posting June-quarter records and the installed base reaching an all-time high across every product category and geography. Layered on top is an optionality story — Siri AI relaunched at WWDC26, plus reported 2027 pipeline items (foldable iPhone, camera-equipped AirPods) — that lets bulls (per the HSBC and BofA/Morgan Stanley commentary in the headlines) argue for further upside even after a 47.4% one-year run to a fresh high (current price is only 1.0% off the 52-week high).

For this to work, two things have to hold that the dossier does not yet confirm: Services growth needs to keep re-accelerating despite the EU forcing Apple to revamp App Store fees under the Digital Markets Act (one headline already flags App Store revenue softness), and the June-quarter's 50.1% gross margin and 29% EPS growth — both inflated by a one-time ~2pp / $0.11 tariff-refund benefit — need to be replaced by organic margin expansion once that comparison rolls off, rather than exposing a much lower underlying growth rate.

## Risks

- **Tariff-refund comp lapses**: Q3 FY26 gross margin of 50.1% and diluted EPS of $2.02 (+29% YoY) each included a one-off benefit (~2pp margin, $0.11 EPS); the next quarter's YoY growth rate will mechanically decelerate once this rolls off, and the dossier gives no guidance figure to size the drop.
- **R&D spend outrunning revenue**: R&D rose from $8,866M to $11,729M year over year (+32%) versus total net sales growth of 16% in the same quarter — a widening gap that only pays off if products like the reported foldable iPhone/AirPods (targeted 2027) ship and sell; any delay compresses operating leverage.
- **Services regulatory pressure**: the EU's forced App Store fee/rule changes under the DMA, combined with a headline reporting App Store revenue declines and disappointing guidance, threaten the highest-margin segment (Services cost of sales was $21,765M on $91,728M nine-month Services revenue, a materially better cost ratio than Products) — a sustained Services deceleration would hit blended net margin (currently 27.6% TTM).

- **Multiple sits above its own growth rate**: P/E of 35.7 against TTM revenue growth of 14.2% and net margin of 27.6% leaves little room for the market's -1.0% 52-week-high positioning to survive a growth miss, especially with annualized volatility already at 28.1%.
