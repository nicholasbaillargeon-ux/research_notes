## Thesis

The market is pricing Apple as a company that has decoupled its growth rate from its size: TTM revenue of $466.82B is still compounding at 14.2%, the June quarter grew 16% to $109.4B with records in iPhone, Mac and Services and in "every geographic segment" per management's own language, and Siri's AI relaunch at WWDC26 is being read as the trigger for a hardware upgrade cycle rather than a defensive move against competitors' AI products. At a $4.54T market cap and price 1.0% off the 52-week high, the stock is being underwritten by the belief that the June-quarter momentum — 29% EPS growth, a new all-time high in active installed base "across all major product categories and geographic segments" — is durable, not a one-off.

For that to work, Apple has to show the June quarter's growth held up ex the tariff-refund benefit: gross margin of 50.1% included "approximately 2 percentage points" from tariff refunds and diluted EPS of $2.02 included $0.11 from the same source, meaning underlying gross margin was closer to 48% and underlying EPS growth was below the 29% headline. The thesis needs Services (up 12.1% YoY to $30.7B in the quarter, 91.7B over nine months) and the AI-refreshed iPhone cycle to carry margin and revenue growth once that refund tailwind laps out of the comps, without R&D spend — up 32.3% YoY to $11.7B in the quarter versus 16% revenue growth — eating further into operating leverage.

## Risks

- **Tariff-refund margin inflation reverses**: the 8-K explicitly quantifies a ~2pp gross-margin and $0.11 EPS benefit from tariff refunds in the June quarter; absent a repeat, gross margin and EPS growth roll over toward the underlying run-rate, and the 29% YoY EPS growth print won't recur on a like-for-like basis.
- **R&D growth outpacing revenue growth**: R&D rose 32.3% YoY ($8,866M to $11,729M) against 16% total revenue growth in the same quarter; if this ratio persists, operating margin compresses even as gross margin holds, since SG&A also grew (10.5% YoY) faster than in prior periods.
