## Thesis

The market is pricing Microsoft as a company whose reported growth is decelerating and whose earnings quality is deteriorating, even though the underlying print says otherwise: FY26 revenue grew 18% to $331.8B, Q4 revenue accelerated to $90.0B (+18%), Azure grew 43%, commercial remaining performance obligation jumped 84% YoY to $678B, and Microsoft 365 Copilot crossed 30 million paid seats. The bull case the tape isn't reflecting is that this backlog (RPO +84%) already underwrites several years of Intelligent Cloud growth, and that the stock's 21% one-year decline and 28.6% gap to its 52-week high have pushed multiples (26.8x TTM P/E, 10.8x P/S) to what one headline in the dossier calls decade lows against a business still compounding revenue at high-teens rates with a 40.3% net margin.

For that thesis to work, two things have to hold: first, the $678B RPO must convert into revenue at margins comparable to today's — Q4 operating income grew 18%, in line with revenue, so leverage has not yet been demonstrated to improve as AI infrastructure scales; second, the GAAP EPS growth Microsoft is reporting (32% for FY26, 32% for Q4) needs to stop depending on one-off marks — Q4 GAAP net income of $35.8B included a $3.2B Anthropic gain and a swing in OpenAI-related impact of over $2B versus the prior year, while non-GAAP net income growth was a materially lower 22%. If the RPO doesn't convert cleanly or the non-GAAP/GAAP gap persists as the "real" growth rate, the current multiple has further to compress, not less.

## Risks

- **Institutional de-risking already visible**: a British hedge fund "slashed" an $8B stake and "almost ended" a decade-long holding per the dossier headlines — this is a concrete, sizeable position exit, not sentiment; further 13F disclosures showing similar reductions would confirm the trend rather than a one-off rebalancing.
- **Earnings quality gap between GAAP and non-GAAP**: Q4 GAAP net income grew 31% to $35.8B versus non-GAAP growth of only 22% to $35.3B, with the difference driven by a $3.2B Anthropic gain and an OpenAI-related swing of roughly $2.06B YoY; FY26 shows the same pattern (32% GAAP EPS growth vs. 22% non-GAAP). If these investment-related gains don't recur next fiscal year, reported EPS growth mechanically resets toward the mid-20s or lower.
- **More Personal Computing is contracting**: segment revenue fell 4% (-5% constant currency) in Q4, with Windows OEM/Devices down 7% and Xbox content/services down 10%, compounded by severance expense and Xbox impairment charges this quarter — the dossier gives no evidence of a floor in this segment, and continued declines would be a direct drag on consolidated growth.
- **Reported-vs-adjusted Microsoft 365 Commercial growth diverges**: reported growth was 14% versus 16% "adjusted" for a prior-year comparable that had benefited from 2 points of in-period revenue recognition — this shows the headline commercial cloud number is sensitive to comp effects, meaning quarter-to-quarter deceleration signals need to be read against that base-effect noise rather than taken at face value.
- **Valuation re-rating without a stated capex offset**: the stock is down 20.1% over six months and trading 28.6% below its 52-week high with annualized volatility up to 25.8%, even as reported growth accelerated — the dossier contains no capital expenditure or free-cash-flow figures, so there is no way to confirm or rule out whether the selloff reflects concerns about AI infrastructure spending intensity eating into the margin story; that absence is itself a gap an investor would need filled before underwriting the "backlog will convert cleanly" thesis.
