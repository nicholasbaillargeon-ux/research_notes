## Risks

- **Multiple compression outrunning earnings growth**: diluted EPS TTM grew to $17.96 (+32% for FY26 GAAP) while the stock fell 21% over one year and 28.6% off its 52-week high — a P/E of 26.8 that's already been rerated down; further compression here is a valuation risk independent of operating results, and a headline explicitly flags "decade lows" in valuation.
- **Earnings quality inflated by one-time items**: Q4 GAAP EPS of $4.81 included a $0.27 per-share net benefit from discrete items — a $3.2B Anthropic gain and lower-than-expected Voluntary Retirement Program costs — partially offset by Xbox severance and impairment charges; non-GAAP EPS excluding OpenAI impact was $4.74, and the OpenAI investment swung net income by $(2,055)M year-over-year, showing how volatile "GAAP" profitability has become at Microsoft's scale.
- **Backlog-to-revenue conversion risk**: commercial RPO of $678B (+84%) is a forward commitment, not booked revenue; any slowdown in conversion pace — not visible in this dossier — would leave the market's growth assumptions unsupported.
- **Legacy segment drag**: More Personal Computing revenue fell 4% (Windows OEM -7%, Xbox content -10%), showing that not every part of the business is participating in the AI-driven acceleration, and Xbox impairment charges specifically hit Q4 GAAP results.
- **Large-holder de-risking and volatility**: a British hedge fund reportedly slashed an $8B stake, and annualized volatility has risen to 25.8% — a flow/sentiment risk layered on top of the fundamental debate, with the stock down 3.17% in a single recent session per the headlines.
